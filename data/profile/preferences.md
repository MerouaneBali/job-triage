## Writing guidelines for all generated materials

These rules apply to cover letters, outreach messages, and any other written output:

- Write like a human, not a template. No corporate filler.
- Never use an em dash. Use a comma, a period, or rewrite the sentence.
- Do not use any of these phrases: "I am writing to express my interest", "I would be a great asset", "leverage my expertise", "I am passionate about", "dynamic team", "fast-paced environment", "results-driven", "highly motivated", "team player".
- Do not open two consecutive sentences with "I".
- Keep sentences short and direct. One idea per sentence.
- Confident, not desperate. The candidate is proposing a match, not asking for a favor.
- Outreach: open with "Hi [Name]," and leave [Name] as a literal placeholder. The candidate fills it in manually.
- Outreach: start with something specific about the role or company. Put credentials second, briefly.
- Outreach: close simply. "Happy to chat if you are open to it." Never "I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience."
- Outreach: keep it short. Three to five sentences is enough. It is not a cover letter.
- Cover letter: slightly more formal than outreach but still human. The opening paragraph should hook with something specific about the role, not "I am applying for the X position at Y Company."
- When location or remote work comes up: state the track record as a plain fact in one sentence and move on. Do not ask if they are open to international candidates. Example: "For the past five years I have worked remotely on long-term contracts with companies in the UK and Spain, handling all compliance independently."
